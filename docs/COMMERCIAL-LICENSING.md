# Commercial licensing

The public release uses a custom source-available license. Its full
[LICENSE](../LICENSE) controls; this guide explains the intended business policy.

## When payment is required

A separate written, paid commercial license is required before selling, renting,
or supplying a paid copy of the advisor or a modified version. This includes paid
bundles. It is also required before offering customers paid hosted access to the
advisor's code-advice or analysis functionality, including subscription access.

Free use includes personal projects, education, research, internal company use,
and use as a development tool for unrelated paid work. Salary or client payment
does not, by itself, trigger payment. Supplying the advisor itself as a paid product
or service does trigger the restriction, even if a charge is described as an
installation or customization fee.

## Arrange payment before monetizing

Contact the repository owner using their GitHub profile's contact channel. There
is no published checkout, fixed price, or automatic royalty calculation.

The authorized rights holder and commercial user must agree in writing on:

- The named parties and the rights holder's authority to grant the license.
- The product, version, modifications, deployment and distribution rights covered.
- The agreed license fee, currency, payment dates and any renewal terms.
- Any limits on customers, users, installations or hosted services.
- Support, warranties, termination and applicable legal terms, if agreed.

An inquiry, a fork, attribution or an unsolicited donation does not grant a
commercial license. Commercial permission arises from the separate written
agreement and its conditions, including the payment terms.

## Rights and contributions

The repository publisher must hold the necessary rights or have authorization
from their holders to offer commercial licenses. Permission to publish code and
authority to grant commercial rights should cover this licensing model.
Third-party dependencies retain their own licenses.

Before accepting outside contributions that will be commercially licensed, settle
the necessary contributor permissions with legal advice. Posting a contribution
under the public license does not automatically authorize the publisher to sell
broader rights to that contributor's copyrighted additions.

## Release history and legal review

Release 0.2.0 introduces these terms. The new license expressly preserves previously
granted permissions for earlier copies; it does not retroactively restrict an
earlier MIT release.

This bespoke license implements the chosen business policy and has not been
reviewed by a lawyer. Have a software-licensing lawyer review both it and the
separate commercial agreement before relying on them for revenue or enforcement.
The public license requires an agreement; it does not itself collect payments.

Commercial restrictions mean the project is source-available, not OSI-approved
open source. See the [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd).
